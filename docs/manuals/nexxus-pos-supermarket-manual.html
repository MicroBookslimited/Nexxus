Chapter 1Before you begin

What the system does, who uses which part of it, and what you need in place before the first sale.

1.1 What NEXXUS POS gives a supermarket

NEXXUS POS is a web-based point of sale. It runs in a browser on a till PC, laptop or tablet — there is nothing to install — and every till in every branch works from the same live catalogue, stock figures and price list.

The parts of the system a supermarket uses every day are:

The lane

A scan-first checkout screen built for speed: scan, take payment, print, next customer.

The catalogue

Products, barcodes, prices, tax status and stock on hand, shared by every till.

The scale station

Weigh loose goods, print a barcoded weight label, and let the lane scan it.

Cash control

Shift floats, payouts, blind counts and over/short at the end of every shift.

Purchasing

Supplier orders and purchase bills that receive stock and update cost prices.

Reporting

Daily sales, GCT/tax, category and hourly performance, staff and stock variance.

1.2 Who does what

PersonWhat they normally do in NEXXUS
Owner / AdminSets up the business, tax, receipts and branches; creates staff; sees every report; manages the subscription.
ManagerRuns the floor: approves discounts and voids, closes other people's shifts, manages products, prices and purchasing, reviews reports.
SupervisorApproves lane overrides, opens and closes shifts, handles refunds and voids.
CashierOpens a shift, sells, holds and recalls bills, closes their own shift.
Inventory clerkReceives goods, counts stock, prints shelf labels, keeps the catalogue tidy.

1.3 What you need on site

  • A reliable internet connection. The lane keeps working through short outages (see §7.13), but setup, reporting and gift vouchers need a connection.
  • One PC, laptop or tablet per lane, running a current browser (Chrome or Edge recommended).
  • A USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner configured as a keyboard wedge — that is, it types the barcode and presses Enter. This is the default for almost every scanner sold.
  • A receipt printer (58 mm or 80 mm) installed as a normal printer on the till.
  • For loose goods: a weighing scale at the deli/produce counter and a label printer, or a scale that prints its own EAN-13 weight-embedded labels.
  • Optional: cash drawer, customer-facing display, second screen.

Chapter 2Opening your account and first-time setup

From sign-up to a working till, in one sitting.

2.1 The setup wizard

New accounts are created at the sign-up page and walked through seven steps:

AccountBusiness Name, Your Name, Email Address, Password (at least 8 characters), accept the Terms & Conditions. This email becomes your login and receives all system mail.
BusinessPhone Number, Business Address, Country and Timezone. The timezone drives every date and time on receipts and reports — set it correctly now.
PlanMonthly or annual billing (annual saves 17%), then the plan. Every account starts on a 3-day free trial.
PaymentPayPal or card. Skipped entirely on the Free plan.
SetupCreate the owner's admin PIN (4–8 digits, entered twice). The owner becomes the first staff member with the Admin role.
HardwareRegister the tills, printers and scanners you already have, download drivers, or choose Just give me the app and do it later.
LaunchConfirmation, and a shortcut straight into the POS.

2.2 Signing in each day

  1. Open the NEXXUS POS address in the browser and sign in with the business email and password. This is the store login — normally done once per device, and the till stays signed in.
  2. Each member of staff then identifies themselves with their own 4–8 digit PIN. Switching cashier does not require signing the store out — use Switch User in the header.
Why two logins
The store login proves the device belongs to your business. The PIN records who made each sale, approved each discount and counted each drawer. Never share PINs — the audit trail is only as good as the PINs behind it.

2.3 Add the till to the home screen

NEXXUS POS can be installed as an app from the browser menu (Install or Add to Home Screen). It then opens full-screen without browser tabs, which stops cashiers from wandering off to other sites and makes offline mode more reliable.

Chapter 3Configuring the store

Everything in this chapter lives under the gear icon → Settings. Work down the list once; after that you will rarely return except for prices and staff.

3.1 The order to do it in

Settings is divided into sections down the left: Business, Industry & Features, Receipt, Currency, Email, Notifications, Inventory, POS Security, POS Interface, Optional Modules, Payment Methods, QR Code, Admin Users, Automation, Integrations, Roles. For a supermarket, complete them in this order: Business → Industry → Receipt (tax) → Currency → POS Interface → POS Security → Inventory → Notifications → Roles → Payment Methods.

Set Business Type to Supermarket. This tunes the default receipt template and the feature flags offered elsewhere. Restaurant-only features (Tables, Kitchen) disappear from the menu.

3.5 Currency

Set the Base Currency (JMD by default). A Secondary Currency with an exchange rate can be shown alongside it — useful for tourist-area stores.

3.6 Choosing the supermarket layout

Under POS Interface the checkout screen can take one of several shapes. They are mutually exclusive — turn on exactly one:

After changing the layout, open POS again to see it.

3.7 Lane security (POS Security)

Supermarket Mode in this section is the anti-shrinkage control. With it on, a cashier must get a manager, supervisor or admin PIN before they can:

  • reduce the quantity of a line,
  • remove a line from the bill,
  • clear the whole bill.

Increasing a quantity never needs approval. Discounts and price overrides always require a manager PIN, whether or not this setting is on.

Recommended
Turn this on. The most common till fraud is scanning goods, voiding lines after the customer has paid cash, and pocketing the difference.

Kiosk lock, elsewhere in settings, stops staff leaving full-screen mode without a manager PIN.

3.8 Inventory behaviour

Allow OversellingIf on, the lane will sell an item whose stock shows zero. Supermarkets normally leave this on — a wrong stock figure should never stop a queue — and fix the count later.
Stock MethodFIFO (first in, first out) or LIFO, used for costing.

3.9 Alerts and the daily digest

Under Notifications:

  • Daily Email Digest — yesterday's trading summary to a chosen address at a chosen time. Use Send Test Email Now to confirm it arrives.
  • Low Stock Alerts — enable, set the recipient, the alert time and the Low Stock Threshold. The system checks hourly and emails the buyer.

3.10 Branches and stations

Locations holds your branches. New Location captures Branch Name, Address and Phone. Each branch has its own Inventory tab and Transfer History; use Init All Products to give a new branch a stock row for every product.

There is no separate screen for tills. A cashier picks the Station / Till Number when opening their cash shift, which is what ties a sale to lane 1, 2 or 3.

3.11 Hardware register

My Hardware is an asset register: record each receipt printer, scanner, cash drawer, card reader, customer display, label printer and tablet with make, model, serial, purchase date and condition. The Driver Downloads tab keeps installer links per platform so a replacement till can be set up quickly.

Chapter 4Staff, roles and permissions

Every action at the lane is tied to a PIN. Get this right and the reports tell you the truth.

4.1 Adding a member of staff

  1. Go to Staff → Add Staff.
  2. Enter Full Name.
  3. Enter a PIN of 4–8 digits. It must be unique across all staff — no two people may share one. Leaving the PIN blank when editing keeps the existing one.
  4. Choose the Role (see the table below).
  5. Tick Can receive cash for anyone who will hold a drawer.
  6. Save. To restrict someone to certain branches, open Branch Access and tick the branches, marking one as Primary. Leaving all unticked means all branches.

When someone leaves, edit them and untick Active rather than deleting — their sales history stays intact.

4.2 The standard roles

RoleTypical use in a supermarket
AdminOwner and IT. Everything, including settings and staff.
ManagerStore manager. Everything except changing system settings and creating staff.
SupervisorFloor supervisor. Sells, approves discounts, voids and refunds, opens and closes shifts.
CashierSells, opens their own shift, views orders and customers. Cannot void or refund.
Inventory ClerkReceiving and counting: full inventory access, reports, and the scale.
AccountantRead-only across orders, inventory, reports and customers, plus exports.
View OnlyLook, don't touch — for auditors and trainees.

Roles can be edited and new ones created under Settings → Roles, where each permission is a tick box. Appendix A lists the full matrix.

Chapter 5Building your product catalogue

The catalogue is the single biggest job in setting up a supermarket, and the one that decides how fast your lanes run.

5.1 What a product record holds

NameWhat the cashier and the receipt see. Include size: Milk 1L, not Milk.
BarcodeThe EAN/UPC printed on the pack. This is what makes the lane fast — every scannable item needs one.
SKUYour own code. Useful for loose goods and store-packed items.
CategoryDrives the Category Sales report and shelf reporting. Keep the list short and consistent.
PriceShelf price. Whether tax is inside it depends on your Tax Mode (§3.4).
CostPurchase cost, used for profit reporting. Updated automatically when you receive a purchase bill.
Attracts sales taxUntick for zero-rated basics. The lane then prints those lines as tax-exempt.
StockQuantity on hand. Per branch when you use Locations.
Sold by weightTurns the item into a loose good — see §5.4.
Selling unit / sizeOptional label such as each, pack, 500g, shown on the receipt.

5.2 Importing an existing catalogue

Do not type thousands of products. Products → Import takes a CSV:

  1. Download a template — a generic one, or the Loyverse or QuickBooks layout if you are moving from those systems.
  2. Fill it in from your supplier price file or old system export.
  3. Upload it. NEXXUS matches the columns automatically; check the mapping for name, price, category, barcode, SKU, stock, in stock and selling unit, and correct anything mismatched.
  4. Import, then read the row-by-row result list and fix the rejects.
Before importing
De-duplicate the file on barcode. Two products sharing one barcode will make the lane ask the cashier which one they meant — every single time it is scanned.

5.3 Categories

Categories power the Category Sales report and make the catalogue searchable. Aim for 15–30 (Produce, Meat, Dairy, Frozen, Bakery, Dry Goods, Beverages, Household, Personal Care, Baby, Pet, Tobacco…) rather than hundreds.

5.4 Loose and weighed goods

Anything sold by weight — meat, produce, deli, bulk rice — is set up as a normal product with Sold by weight turned on and a unit of lb, kg, oz or g. The price is the price per that unit. Two things then change:

  • At the lane, choosing the item asks for a weight instead of a quantity, and the line reads e.g. 3.750 lb.
  • At the scale station the item can be weighed and given a printed barcode label that the lane simply scans (Chapter 8).

5.5 Bundles and multi-buys

A product can be built as a composite — a hamper or a "three for" pack made of other products. Selling the bundle draws stock from the components. For simple date-limited price cuts, use Promotions (§10.2) instead.

5.6 Shelf and product labels

The printer icon on a product card opens Print Label: choose Small, Medium or Large, the quantity to print, and whether to show store name, price, category and the barcode number. A CODE128 barcode is generated; if a product has no barcode of its own, NEXXUS prints one based on the product number so it can still be scanned.

5.7 Watching for gaps

The Products page shows live running low and out of stock counts. Click either badge to list exactly which lines are affected, then edit or restock from there.

Chapter 6Buying stock: suppliers, orders and bills

Two documents: an order is what you asked for, a bill is what arrived. Only the bill moves stock.

6.1 Suppliers

Create each supplier once with name, contact, phone, email, website, address and notes. Purchase documents then link to the supplier record, so Payables can show what you owe each one.

6.2 Raising a purchase order

  1. Products → Orders tab → New Purchase Order.
  2. Choose the supplier and add products with quantities, expected cost and tax.
  3. Set the expected date and save as a draft; when you send it to the supplier, mark it Sent.

An order changes nothing in stock or accounts. It exists so you can check the delivery against what you ordered.

6.3 Receiving a delivery

  1. Products → Purchases tab → New Purchase Bill (or convert the matching purchase order).
  2. Enter the supplier's invoice number, date and lines — quantity received and the cost actually charged, with per-line tax if the invoice is taxed.
  3. For perishables with batch tracking enabled, record the batch/lot number and expiry date.
  4. Check the total against the supplier's invoice, then Confirm & Receive.

Confirming does three things: it adds the goods to stock, updates each product's cost price, and raises the amount owed in Payables.

Get it right first time
Confirm only when the delivery has been checked in. A confirmed bill has moved stock and money; correcting it afterwards is far more work than fixing a draft.

6.4 Returns to supplier

Damaged or short-dated goods going back to the supplier are recorded as a supplier return, which removes the stock and reduces what you owe.

Chapter 7Running the checkout lane

This chapter is the cashier's chapter. Print Appendix C and tape it beside the till.

7.1 Getting the lane open

Opening POS presents up to two gates:

  1. Staff PIN Required — the cashier types their 4–8 digit PIN.
  2. No Open Shift — a full-screen message with Open Shift in Cash Management. No one can sell without an open cash shift; see §9.1.

With both cleared, the header shows Supermarket Mode and the station number, and the lane is ready.

7.2 The lane at a glance

Left — CURRENT BILL: the running list of items with Item, Qty and Amount. When empty it reads "Scan an item to begin."

Right — the scan box and keypad: the box is labelled Scan or type SKU / barcode (or Search by name, SKU, or barcode in search mode). Below it sit the number keypad, totals and the payment buttons.

Header buttons: Add Customer, Hold, Recall (with a badge showing how many bills are parked), Clear, Dashboard, reload, Switch User, Lock, and the X that closes the shift.

Keep the cursor in the scan box
The lane keeps focus in the scan box on purpose. If scanning stops registering, click once in the box and carry on.

7.3 Scanning

Scan the barcode; the scanner types it and presses Enter, and the item drops onto the bill. You can also type a barcode or SKU by hand and press Enter.

  • Unknown code — "No product found." Try the item's SKU, or search by name if your lane has search mode. In search mode a genuinely new line can be added on the spot with Add "…" as a new product.
  • Two products share a barcode — the lane asks which one; pick the right one and have the office fix the duplicate.
  • Search mode — type part of a name and up to eight matches appear with barcode and price; click one to add it.

7.4 Quantities

Three ways, in order of speed:

  1. Multiple of the same item: type the number on the keypad before scanning. The keypad shows × qty and the next scan is added that many times. Six tins: press 6, scan one tin.
  2. Adjust a line: use the + and − buttons on the line.
  3. Set exactly: tap the line to select it, key the number, press Set Quantity.

C clears the keypad and the backspace key deletes the last digit. Weighed lines show a fixed decimal quantity and have no +/− buttons — remove and re-add to change them.

7.5 Weighed goods at the lane

Scale label: scan the label printed at the deli or produce counter. It is an EAN-13 starting with 2 that carries the product and the weight, so the line arrives complete — no popup, no typing. Each label can only be sold once.

No label: select the loose product; the lane asks for the weight, then adds a line such as Beef Stew Cut — 3.750 lb.

Two rules for scale labels
A bill containing a scale label cannot be held. And if a label is removed from the bill, or the bill is cleared, the label is released and can be scanned again at another lane.

7.6 Price check

Price Check looks up any item without touching the bill or stock — scan or type the code and the screen shows the product, unit, size, the price in large type, and whether tax is included, added or exempt. Use it for shelf-edge queries mid-queue.

7.7 Attaching a customer

Add Customer searches by name, phone or email, or adds a new one on the spot. A customer is required for a Credit sale and for any remainder put on account in a split. Attaching a customer also builds their purchase history and loyalty record.

7.8 Discounts

  1. Press Discount.
  2. A manager, supervisor or admin enters their PIN on the Manager Override Required screen.
  3. Choose Percent or Fixed $, enter the value, press Apply Discount.

The discount shows in the totals with the approver recorded against it, and it is applied before tax. Remove it with the X. Held bills keep their discount when recalled. Every discount appears in the Discounts & Voids report — review it weekly.

7.9 Holding and recalling a bill

Customer forgot the milk? Press Hold. The bill is parked, the lane clears, and the next customer can be served. Recall lists the parked bills with their totals and item counts; tap one, confirm that it replaces what is on screen, and it comes back — discount and all.

Bills with scale labels or parcel pickups cannot be held.

7.10 Taking payment

Four buttons: Cash, Card, Credit, Split.

TenderHow it works
CashPress Cash, then key what the customer handed over — or tap a quick button (exact amount, or the sensible rounded notes). Change Due is displayed in large type.
CardPress Card and choose Debit or Credit; the choice is required because it separates the two on your banking report. Take the payment on the terminal as usual, then complete the sale.
CreditPuts the whole sale on the customer's account. A customer must be selected first, and the balance appears in Receivables.
SplitOpens a panel where you enter the card amount (with card type), the cash amount, and anything left over goes on account. Card plus cash may not exceed the total, and an on-account remainder needs a customer. The summary reads Tap to edit split.

7.11 Gift vouchers

Enter the code in the Voucher code box and press Apply. NEXXUS checks it is valid, not cancelled, not expired and has a balance, then shows the code, its balance, the amount applied and the amount still due. A voucher is a tender, not a discount: it pays part of the bill. If it covers everything, the payment buttons dim and the checkout button reads CHECKOUT · Voucher $…. Otherwise take the remainder in cash or card as normal.

Offline
Vouchers need a live connection — they cannot be validated or redeemed while the lane is offline.

7.12 Finishing the sale

Press the large CHECKOUT · $amount button. It is blocked if the bill is empty, a split does not add up, a customer is missing for credit, or a card type has not been chosen.

Sale Complete then shows the order number, subtotal, tax and total — plus cash tendered and change due on a cash sale. Print Receipt reprints; Done clears the lane for the next customer. With auto-print on, the receipt is already coming out of the printer.

Double charges
Every checkout carries a one-time reference, so a slow response, an impatient second press or a dropped connection cannot turn one sale into two.

7.13 When the internet drops

A banner appears: "You're offline — changes will sync automatically when your connection returns." The lane keeps serving from what it has already loaded and queues each sale. When the connection returns the queue is sent automatically and the banner clears.

  • Do not close the browser or shut the till down while the banner is showing — the queue lives on that device.
  • Gift vouchers are unavailable offline.
  • Stock figures and prices are as they were when the connection dropped.

7.14 Refunds, voids and reprints

Completed sales live in Order List. Find the order (scan the barcode on the receipt if you enabled it), then reprint the receipt, void it, or refund the whole sale or individual lines. Refunded stock goes back on hand automatically. Voids and refunds need the right permission — normally supervisor and above — and every one is listed in the Discounts & Voids report and the audit trail.

Chapter 8The weighing scale station

Deli, butchery and produce: weigh once at the counter, scan at the lane.

8.1 Printing a weight label

  1. Open Weighing Scale. Only products marked sold by weight appear here.
  2. Find the item with Search by name, barcode or PLU….
  3. Put the goods on the scale and enter the weight — typed, keyed on the on-screen keypad, or sent straight from a USB scale.
  4. Optionally set Pack Date and Expiration Date for fresh goods.
  5. Press Generate & Print Label. The label prints with the weight, the price and a barcode.

8.2 Active labels

The Active Labels panel lists every printed label not yet sold, with its weight and total price. Each can be reprinted (if the sticker is damaged) or voided (if the goods went back). Labels disappear from the list once the lane sells them.

End-of-day habit
Void the labels still showing at close for goods that were never sold, so tomorrow's list is clean.

8.3 Managing which products are weighed

Manage Products on the same page lets you search the catalogue and switch sold by weight and the unit on or off without leaving the counter.

Chapter 9Cash control and end of day

A shift is the accounting unit of the lane: one cashier, one drawer, one count.

9.1 Opening a shift

  1. Open Cash Mgmt and identify yourself with your PIN.
  2. Choose the Branch/Location and the Station / Till Number, then Continue.
  3. Count the float into the drawer and enter it as the opening cash. Submit.

The lane unlocks. The active shift panel shows Opening Cash, cash sales, payouts and Expected Cash, calculated as Opening + Cash Sales − Payouts.

9.2 Money leaving the drawer during the shift

Any cash taken out — paying a supplier, petty cash, a bank drop — is recorded with Add Payout: the amount and a reason, which is required. Payouts are listed on the shift and subtracted from expected cash. Cash coming in is simply the cash you take in sales.

9.3 Closing the shift

  1. Press Close Shift.
  2. Count the drawer and enter what is actually there. Enter the denomination breakdown (notes and coins) if your store requires one, then Confirm & Close.
  3. The screen shows Expected from System against Actual Cash Counted and the difference — over or short.

The count is entered before the variance is settled, so cashiers cannot count "towards" the expected figure. Investigate anything beyond your tolerance the same day, while people still remember the shift.

9.4 Manager close and stuck shifts

A manager or admin can close a shift for a cashier who has gone home — the close dialog shows an admin banner. If a till crashed mid-shift and the shift will not close, an admin can use Force Close Stuck Session.

9.5 The end-of-day report

After closing, the EOD detail breaks the shift down — cash sales, card, credit, split components, voucher takings, refunds, payouts, opening float and expected cash. Print EOD Report for the cash bag, or download it for the office.

Chapter 10Prices and promotions

Move a whole category's prices in one pass, and run date-limited specials without touching the base price.

10.1 Bulk price updates

  1. Open Price Manager → Bulk Update.
  2. Pick what to update: All Products, By Category or Specific Products.
  3. Choose the adjustment: a percentage, a markup on cost, or a fixed amount.
  4. Choose rounding — none, or to the nearest $5, $10, $50, $100 or $1000 — so shelf prices stay tidy.
  5. Press Preview changes and read the old-versus-new list carefully.
  6. Apply. Every change is written to the History tab with who did it and when.

10.2 Promotions

Promotions → New Promotion: pick the product, the promo price and the start and end date-and-time. The list shows the regular price struck through beside the promo price, and whether the promotion is upcoming, active or expired. While it is active the lane charges the promo price automatically; when it expires the price returns on its own with no action needed.

Chapter 11Customers, credit and gift vouchers

11.1 Customer records

Customers holds name, phone, email, address and notes, each with a loyalty card number, purchase history and account balance. Customers can be created at the lane while serving.

11.2 Selling on account

A Credit sale (or the on-account leg of a split) posts the amount to the customer's balance in Receivables, where you can see who owes what and record payments as they come in. Only sell on account to customers you have agreed terms with.

11.3 Gift vouchers

Gift Vouchers issues and manages vouchers: each has a code, a balance, and an optional expiry, and can be cancelled. Redemption happens at the lane (§7.11). A voucher can be spent across several visits until the balance runs out, and a refund can be issued as a new voucher rather than cash.

Chapter 12Stock control after opening

12.1 Counts and adjustments

Stock drifts — breakage, theft, miskeyed deliveries. Count a section at a time (a full-store count means closing the doors) and post the count so the system figure matches the shelf. Every adjustment records the staff member and reason, and the Stock Variance report shows where the losses are concentrated.

12.2 Multi-branch stock

With Locations in use, each branch carries its own stock figures and its own Transfer History. Moving goods between branches is recorded as a transfer with From Branch, To Branch, Product, Quantity and Notes — never as an adjustment at both ends, which loses the audit trail.

12.3 Batches and expiry

Where batch/expiry tracking is enabled, purchase bills capture the lot number and expiry date, which is what lets you find affected stock quickly in a recall and rotate short-dated goods.

12.4 Low stock in practice

Combine three things: the running-low badges on the Products page for a quick look, the emailed low-stock alert for the buyer's morning routine, and the Inventory and Product Sales reports for what actually sells.

13.1 Audit trail

Audit Trail records who did what and when across the system — price changes, voids, refunds, setting changes, staff edits. When a figure looks wrong, start here.

Chapter 14Housekeeping: backups, plan and support

14.1 Backups

Backup exports a copy of your business data and can restore it. Take a backup before any bulk operation — a large import, a bulk price change, a catalogue clean-up.

Restores replace
A restore replaces your current data with the backup's contents. Use it deliberately, not as an undo button for a single mistake.

14.2 Subscription and add-ons

Plan shows your subscription, renewal date and paid add-ons, and is where you upgrade when the catalogue or staff list outgrows your plan.

14.3 Getting help

Support holds step-by-step guides and raises a ticket with the NEXXUS team. Include the till, the time and the order number — it turns a day of back-and-forth into one reply.

Cannot add a new productYou are at the plan's product limit. Archive discontinued lines or upgrade the plan.
Stock figure is obviously wrongCount the section and post an adjustment; then check whether the delivery was received as a confirmed purchase bill.

Appendix BDaily, weekly and monthly routines

Opening the store

  • Sign in on each till and confirm the lane loads.
  • Each cashier opens a shift with a counted float, on their own station number.
  • Check the printer has paper and prints a test receipt.
  • Read the overnight low-stock alert and start the day's ordering.

During the day

  • Record every cash removal as a payout, with a reason, as it happens.
  • Receive deliveries as purchase bills the same day they arrive.
  • Void unsold scale labels rather than leaving them active.

Closing the store

  • Every cashier closes their shift with a counted drawer; investigate variances now, not tomorrow.
  • Print or download the EOD report for the cash bag.
  • Glance at Daily Sales and Payment Methods against the money in hand.

Weekly

  • Read the Discounts & Voids report line by line.
  • Count one or two sections and post adjustments.
  • Check Category Sales and Hourly Sales for rostering and space decisions.
  • Confirm supplier bills are entered and Payables matches the statements.

Monthly

  • Export the GCT/Tax report for filing.
  • Review Profit Snapshot and Stock Variance by department.
  • Take a backup, and archive products you no longer stock.
  • Review staff, roles and PINs — remove leavers.
